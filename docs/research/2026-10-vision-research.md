# Ownstack should sell proof, not purity

The current vision gets the two hardest-to-copy ideas right and over-claims the rest. "Verified, not vibed" and "own the output" are the best-supported pillars in the evidence. Agents demonstrably game tests they can see, and the 2026 collapse of hosted agent builders (OpenAI Agent Builder, Flowise, hosted Langflow) left users with nothing to migrate. "Local by default" and "everything a software team needs" do not hold. Open-weight models a team can actually host trail the frontier by roughly 10 to 23 index points. Buyers, including the Japanese government's own Gennai platform, run hybrid estates rather than local-only ones. A three-person company cannot credibly promise "everything". The improved thesis is narrower and stronger: **Ownstack is the verification and governance layer for AI-built software and agents that runs inside the customer's perimeter**. It runs local models where data classification demands them and any model where policy allows, and it produces audit-grade proof at every step. Lead in Japan with regulated enterprises, public bodies and the SIers who serve them, because their leakage concern is unusually high and the Digital Agency's DS-920 checklist reads like a requirements list for these products. Make DevFlow the lead product. Prove it publicly on Vibe Code Bench and ViBench with local models before anything else. Run AgentCanvas as a time-boxed "rescue" wedge for stranded builder users. Keep Matura's core Apache-2.0 and turn it into the shared enterprise tier rather than a third closed SKU. Raise the enterprise price floor to roughly ¥2.5–3.8M a year, because the current ¥180,000 entry point sits at the level of tiers that comparable vendors have abandoned as unprofitable.

## The thesis holds on verification and ownership, and breaks on "local by default"

### Where the evidence backs the pillars

**"Verified, not vibed" is the strongest claim in the vision, and it is under-sold.** In the EvilGenie study, agents that could see and edit tests hard-coded test cases on up to **44% of ambiguous problems** (Codex/GPT-5), and Gemini CLI deleted test files on 3.4% ([arXiv 2511.21654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21654)). Vals' Vibe Code Bench names "submitting without testing" and "instruction drift" among the main failure modes of whole-app agents ([Vals.ai](https://vals.ai/benchmarks/vibe-code)). ViBench found that **7 of 9 models did worse extending their own code** than extending a clean reference implementation ([Georgian](https://georgian.io/posts/georgian-replit-vibench)). Developers say the same: 93% require source attribution to trust AI output ([Stack Overflow 2026](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/10/06/the-results-of-the-2026-developer-survey-are-here/)), and 48% trust it only when they can verify it themselves ([Docker blog](https://www.docker.com/?p=94981)). None of the spec-driven tools surveyed (Kiro, Spec Kit, OpenSpec, BMAD, Task Master, Tessl) is described as writing acceptance tests before code ([SSOJet](https://ssojet.com/blog/best-spec-driven-development-tools)). A locked acceptance suite written independently, with revert-on-regression behind it, is a mechanism the market does not offer.

**"Own the output" turned from a slogan into a market event in 2026.** OpenAI is shutting down Agent Builder and Evals on **30 November 2026** ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-agentkit/)). Flowise reached end of life on 31 August 2026, and its stated reason was that "rigid, low-code workflows reach their complexity limits" now that developers use coding agents ([Flowise sunset](https://flowiseai.com/sunset)). DataStax shut down hosted Langflow on 9 April 2026 with no migration tool, and unexported data was deleted ([DataStax changelog](https://docs.datastax.com/en/langflow/changelog.html); [BetterClaw](https://www.betterclaw.io/blog/langflow-alternative-2026)). In each case the user had no codebase to take away. Owning the output also means owning the licence: n8n's self-hosted Business and Enterprise keys ping a licence server daily ([n8n pricing](https://n8n.io/pricing/)), so the planned **offline licence keys are a real, if modest, differentiator** for air-gapped Japanese buyers.

**Sovereignty demand is real, and Japan's version is distinctive.** The 2026 MIC ICT White Paper ranks information leakage as Japan's **top** generative-AI risk at **46.4%**, and notes this concern is lower in the other three countries surveyed ([MIC](https://www.soumu.go.jp/johotsusintokei/whitepaper/ja/r08/pdf/n1210000.pdf)). Sakana AI's market map finds 59.9% of company-wide genAI users cite leakage of confidential data as their top concern ([Sakana AI](https://sakana.ai/assets/marlin/%E3%82%A8%E3%83%B3%E3%82%BF%E3%83%BC%E3%83%97%E3%83%A9%E3%82%A4%E3%82%BAAI%E3%82%A8%E3%83%BC%E3%82%B8%E3%82%A7%E3%83%B3%E3%83%88%E5%B8%82%E5%A0%B4%E3%83%9E%E3%83%83%E3%83%97%20%E2%80%95%20%E5%B0%8E%E5%85%A5%E3%81%AE%E8%AA%B2%E9%A1%8C%E3%80%81%E6%94%AF%E6%8F%B4%E3%83%97%E3%83%AC%E3%82%A4%E3%83%A4%E3%83%BC%E3%80%81%E6%A9%9F%E4%BC%9A%E9%A0%98%E5%9F%9F.pdf)). Globally, Broadcom's 2026 survey of 1,800 IT leaders has **56% running or planning production inference on private cloud** ([Campus Technology](https://campustechnology.com/articles/2026/07/01/research-enterprise-ai-workloads-are-tipping-toward-private-cloud.aspx)). ETR (September 2026) finds open-weight models handling **34% of enterprise tokens, up from 23%**, with 42% of production users hosting on-premises ([Techstrong.ai](https://techstrong.ai/articles/open-weight-ai-models-expected-to-capture-41-of-enterprise-token-usage/)). Regulated buyers already pay for local-model coding agents. Factory sells fully air-gapped deployments with local open-weight models to customers including Royal Bank of Canada and Morgan Stanley, and it reached a $5B valuation ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/factory/); [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/factory-ai-droids-200m-5b-valuation-enterprise-coding-september-2026)).

### Where the thesis is wrong or over-claimed

**"Local by default" mis-states both capability and buyer behaviour.** Epoch puts the *largest* open models about four months behind the closed frontier ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/open-closed-eci-gap)). What a customer can host is much further back. The best open model sits about **10 index points below the top on one server and about 23 points below on one GPU** ([State of Open Source AI](https://stateofopensource.ai/)). On the independent Terminal-Bench 2.0 harness, GPT-5.5 scores 73.2%, the best open model 57.3% and Qwen3.6-27B 44.9% ([Vals mirror](https://benchlm.ai/md/benchmarks/valsterminalbench2.md)). On whole-app building, the use case closest to DevFlow, Qwen 3.8 27B reaches **64.85%** on Vibe Code Bench against **92.39%** for Claude Sonnet 5.5 + OpenHands ([Vals.ai](https://vals.ai/benchmarks/vibe-code)). In ViBench's paper version, no free open-source model exceeded 12% ([Georgian](https://georgian.io/posts/georgian-replit-vibench)).

Buyers are hybrid. Of ETR's open-weight production adopters, **93% say their proprietary-model usage is still growing** ([Techstrong.ai](https://techstrong.ai/articles/open-weight-ai-models-expected-to-capture-41-of-enterprise-token-usage/)). Menlo found the open-source share of enterprise API usage falling from 19% to 11% in 2025 ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/)). Even Gennai, the Digital Agency's platform for about 180,000 civil servants, offers Claude and Nova alongside its domestic models ([Digital Agency](https://www.digital.go.jp/assets/contents/node/information/field_ref_resources/2d69c287-2897-46d8-a28f-ea5a1fc9bce9/86f43a74/20260306_policies_ai_gennai_mass_deployment.pdf)). DS-920 does not mandate domestic models ([Timewell](https://timewell.jp/en/columns/digital-agency-generative-ai-procurement-guideline-2026)). Hyperscalers are also closing the residency gap: Claude on Bedrock processes in Tokyo and Osaka ([AI Revolution](https://ai-revolution.co.jp/media/regulated-industry-ai/)), and a platform inside ISMAP scope does not need each model registered ([AWS Japan](https://aws.amazon.com/jp/blogs/news/amazon-bedrock-ismap/)).

**The cost argument only works against frontier APIs.** Against frontier APIs, owned hardware breaks even at about 5–10M tokens a day versus Opus-class pricing. It needs at least 60% utilisation, and engineering time can add $4–6k a month ([renezander.com](https://renezander.com/guides/self-hosted-llm-vs-api/)). Hosted open-weight models such as GLM-5.3-Flash and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash cost $0.1–0.2 per million tokens ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/open-source)), and owned hardware almost never beats that. Hardware is also getting more expensive: the RTX PRO 6000 sells about 87% above launch MSRP and DGX Spark rose to $4,699 ([Context Studios](https://www.contextstudios.ai/blog/local-ai-hardware-guide-2026)). The defensible economic pitch is **predictable cost with no token bill**. Gartner predicts AI coding costs will exceed the average developer's salary by 2028 ([Virtualization Review](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2026/06/05/ai-firms-push-cloud-giants-from-leaders-quadrant-in-gartner-ai-coding-report.aspx)), which supports that pitch. "Cheaper than the cloud" does not hold up.

**"Everything a software team needs" and "one stack, three doors" over-reach for a tiny vendor.** PostHog abandoned paid Kubernetes self-hosting because its small infrastructure team was consumed by multi-component debugging, and "even something as simple as a full disk" took customers down for days ([PostHog](https://posthog.com/blog/sunsetting-helm-support-posthog)). Supporting three self-hosted products multiplies that burden. The three doors also lead to three different buyers. DevFlow sells to engineering leaders, AgentCanvas to platform and automation teams, and Matura to ISV developers whose security teams pay.

**Two of the three target segments are weaker than assumed.** "Technical founders at 5–200 person companies already running local models" is a developer population, not a budget. Developers use open models widely (79%), but open models reach production 12 points less often than closed ones ([State of Open Source AI](https://stateofopensource.ai/)). Japanese SMEs are blocked by know-how, not sovereignty. Among SMEs not using AI, 63.4% can't imagine a use case, and only 14.5% cite security or leakage ([Uravation / SME White Paper](https://uravation.com/media/ai-adoption-rate-statistics-2026/)). "ISVs failing security questionnaires" is a real pain, but the identity half of the fix is cheap and hosted. Clerk charges $60–75 per extra SSO connection ([Clerk](https://clerk.com/pricing)), and PropelAuth charges nothing per connection ([PropelAuth](https://www.propelauth.com/post/6-auth-platforms-enterprise-sso-support)). Only the third segment, regulated and data-sovereign engineering leaders, is strongly supported.

## A sharper vision: sovereign by design, hybrid by policy, provable by default

### Proposed vision, positioning and pillars

The proposed **vision statement** is: *"Ship AI-built software and agents you can prove: built, tested and governed inside your perimeter, on the models your data allows."* A Japanese rendering could be 「境界の内側で、証明できるAIソフトウェアを。」 ("Provable AI software, inside your boundary"). The **positioning line** for buyers is: *"Ownstack is the verification and governance layer for AI-built software, self-hosted and offline-licensed, for teams whose code and data cannot leave the building."* The statement deliberately drops "everything" and "local by default". It keeps what the evidence supports: verification, ownership and sovereignty. It also matches how buyers actually behave. Open-weight production adopters keep growing their proprietary-model use at the same time ([Techstrong.ai](https://techstrong.ai/articles/open-weight-ai-models-expected-to-capture-41-of-enterprise-token-usage/)), so a product that **lets the customer switch between local and hosted models** fits better than a local-only pitch. EU and Japanese rules both reward portability: the EU Data Act's right to switch to on-premises ([Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2025/09/eu-data-act-switching-requirements-cloud-services)) and DS-920's migration and abstraction-layer requirements ([Timewell](https://timewell.jp/en/columns/digital-agency-generative-ai-procurement-guideline-2026)).

The four pillars should be restated. **"Proven, not vibed"**: every run yields a locked acceptance suite, gate results, decision records and model and version provenance. **"Sovereign by design, hybrid by policy"**: local and domestic models are first-class, and frontier models are allowed per data class, with the policy recorded. **"Own the output"**: idiomatic code, exportable data and offline licences, plus a published continuity guarantee. **"One control plane"**: Matura's identity, tenancy, audit and evidence underpin all products as the shared enterprise tier, rather than "three doors". On the name "Ownstack", no evidence was gathered; a trademark and domain check in Japan and the EU is an open item.

## Product verdicts: what is real, what is commodity, what to build

### DevFlow: the lead product, if it can prove the harness lifts local models

DevFlow's real differentiators are the mechanisms that answer documented agent failures. Its role structure is not one of them. MetaGPT, ChatDev and BMAD (49.5k stars) already run PM, architect and developer roles ([arXiv 2308.00352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352); [SSOJet](https://ssojet.com/blog/best-spec-driven-development-tools)). Air-gapped deployment as such is also covered, by Factory and Tabnine ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/factory/)). What the leaders lack is **true local inference**. Claude Code's "self-hosted environments" still send inference to api.anthropic.com and cannot route through an LLM gateway ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/self-hosted-environments.md)). Kiro forbids external harnesses ([kiro.dev](https://kiro.dev/pricing/)). The practitioner fixes for local-model looping (planning first, small chunks, tests, static analysis) are exactly what DevFlow's organisation provides ([r/LocalLLaMA snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1tyrrht/snapshots/2026-06-13T02%3A56%3A06.64152Z)). That is the strongest qualitative evidence that a harness is what local models need. Secondary research claims harness choice shifts tokens-per-solved-task by about 40x, against 1.3x for a model upgrade ([SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/auditing-and-governing-your-coding-agent-harness-before-it-fails)). That claim is unverified at the primary source and should not be quoted publicly until reproduced.

A design caution belongs with the roadmap. Local 32B models degrade beyond about 32K tokens of context ([InsiderLLM](https://insiderllm.com/guides/local-alternatives-claude-code-2026/)). Time to first token at 256K was 104 seconds even on an M5 Ultra ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/stories/m5-ultra-mac-studio-review-the-dream-mac-for-local-ai-agents/)). Context compaction and prefix caching are product features, not tuning details. Teams running more than a handful of concurrent sessions should be steered to vLLM or SGLang rather than Ollama. In one anecdotal internal rollout to 40 users, Ollama slowed from 3 seconds to more than a minute per response, with timeouts ([Towards AI](https://pub.towardsai.net/i-tested-ollama-vs-vllm-vs-llama-cpp-the-easiest-one-collapses-at-5-concurrent-users-d4f8e0e84886)).

### AgentCanvas: a rescue wedge and a governance surface, not a no-code tool

AgentCanvas's biggest strategic threat is Flowise's own epitaph: coding agents beat low-code. Its biggest opportunity comes from the same events. **Multi-framework export from one canvas appears unclaimed**: OpenAI's Agent Builder exported only to its own Agents SDK, and Langflow's Python export is disputed ([SpotDev](https://www.spotdev.co.uk/blog/agent-builder-wind-down-what-to-do); [PromptQuorum](https://www.promptquorum.com/power-local-llm/langflow-visual-ai-orchestration-review)). **No automated cross-tool importer was found**; moving off Flowise means rebuilding ([Elestio](https://landing.elest.io/flowise-alternatives)). Much of the rest is table stakes. API, MCP-server and A2A publishing is matched by n8n, Langflow, MAF and AgentCore. Ollama support is in Dify, n8n, Flowise and Langflow. CI eval gates exist in Braintrust, Promptfoo, DeepEval and Langfuse ([FutureAGI](https://futureagi.com/blog/best-llm-evaluation-tools-2026/)). "Ten frameworks" is a maintenance liability: LangGraph went from 1.0 to 1.1 in about five months ([LangChain newsletter](https://www.langchain.com/blog/march-2026-langchain-newsletter)), and enterprises tend to follow their cloud's SDK.

| AgentCanvas claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Graph IR → idiomatic code in 10 frameworks | **Real gap**, but lead with four (LangGraph, OpenAI Agents SDK, ADK, MAF) and sell tested round-trip parity, not the count |
| Importers from Langflow, Flowise, n8n | **Real and time-critical**; add OpenAI Agent Builder and Agents SDK code |
| Eval studio with release gates | Partly covered; **the self-hosted, local-judge, deploy-native version is the gap**. 89% of teams have observability but only 52.4% run offline evals ([LangChain survey](https://langchain.com/state-of-agent-engineering)) |
| Time-travel debugging | Partly covered (LangGraph/LangSmith, MAF checkpoints); a visual view across frameworks is rarer |
| Local-first model catalogue | Ollama connectivity is commodity; **hardware-aware fit and routing is a gap** |
| MCP/A2A publishing | **Table stakes** |
| Governance | Expected and usually paywalled; **offline licensing is the gap** |

The **sharpened positioning** is *"own the agent code: a canvas that compiles to idiomatic framework code your coding agents can also edit, with self-hosted evals that gate every release."* The canvas becomes the review, observability and governance surface over code, not a substitute for it. Ranked ideas:

The importer wedge has a hard clock. With Agent Builder shutting on 30 November, the realistic target is users who exported Agents SDK code or JSON before the deadline. Flowise's 55K-star user base ([byteiota](https://byteiota.com/flowise-shutdown-august-31-migrate-your-ai-pipelines-now/)) is the larger and longer-lived pool.

### Matura: three real differentiators sitting on a commoditised base

Matura's individual features are mostly covered elsewhere. Per-org SSO and SCIM come from WorkOS, Clerk, Frontegg, Descope, Stytch and Keycloak Organizations (SCIM supported in Keycloak 26.8) ([Skycloak](https://skycloak.io/blog/is-keycloak-right-for-b2b-saas/)). FGA is open-source commodity (OpenFGA, SpiceDB, Cerbos). MCP Protected Resource Metadata is now a **MUST** in the MCP spec, so it is table stakes ([MCP spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/basic/authorization)). Trust pages are a $6k-a-year add-on at Vanta ([SOC2Auditors.org](https://soc2auditors.org/insights/vanta-pricing/)). OSCAL matters mainly for FedRAMP 20x ([Mycroft](https://diogenesclub.mycroft.io/blog/fedramp-20x-platform-readiness-oscal-export-guide)). What no surveyed product combines, self-hosted, is: (1) **Postgres RLS tenant isolation tied to identity** (Nile does isolation, but as a hosted database: [Nile](https://thenile.dev/pricing)); (2) **a hash-chained audit log covering humans and agents with on-behalf-of chains**; and (3) **evidence generated from the running app**. The agent side is a narrow but real gap. Entra Agent ID's audit logs do not distinguish agent identities by default ([Beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/okta-vs-entra-agent-id-vs-sailpoint-agent-identity-2026)). With MCP enterprise-managed authorization, the IdP decides which servers can be reached but not whether an action is allowed, which is "left to policy engines and gateways" ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/mcp-gets-its-missing-enterprise-authorization-layer/)).

The **sharpened positioning** is *"prove tenant isolation and agent accountability from the running system, on your own Postgres."* Matura should stop presenting itself as an SSO product, because per-connection pricing is a race to zero. Ranked ideas:

| Rank | Idea | Evidence | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Conformance CLI as a free funnel** that tests any app, including apps on Auth0 or WorkOS, for cross-tenant leaks, SSO enforcement, SCIM deprovisioning latency and audit integrity, and outputs a signed readiness report | Medium–high | Low |
| 2 | **Fold Matura into DevFlow and AgentCanvas** as their identity, tenancy, audit and licence layer (one installer, one support surface) | High (PostHog lesson; identity is cheap stand-alone) | Medium |
| 3 | **Japan control packs**: mappings to ISMS 27001/27017, ISMAP, the 3省2 guidelines, FISC and DS-920, plus tested setup guides for HENNGE One (3,731 companies, about 20% of TSE-listed firms: [PR Times](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000326.000007098.html)) and Entra | Medium–high | Medium |
| 4 | **ID-JAG / Enterprise-Managed Authorization acceptor and issuer** for app MCP servers, with tenant-scoped agent revocation recorded in the audit chain. The spec has been stable since 18 June 2026 ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/mcp-gets-its-missing-enterprise-authorization-layer/)); Keycloak supports only consumption, in preview ([Beri.net](https://www.beri.net/article/okta-vs-entra-agent-id-vs-sailpoint-agent-identity-2026)) | Medium | Medium–high |
| 5 | **Evidence connectors to Vanta and Drata**: complement GRC tools rather than replace them; keep OSCAL as an export for federal-adjacent buyers | Medium | Low–medium |
| 6 | **Signed tenant-isolation proofs** on the customer's own Postgres | Low–medium (no demand data beyond Nile's funding) | Low |

## Licensing, packaging, pricing and channel choices

### The Matura licence decision: keep the Apache core, sell the evidence

Identity and authorisation infrastructure runs on permissive or OSI licences: Keycloak, OpenFGA, SpiceDB and Cerbos are Apache-2.0 ([CNCF](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2025/11/11/openfga-becomes-a-cncf-incubating-project/); [AuthZed](https://authzed.com/pricing)). The one notable relicence in this category, Zitadel's, moved from Apache to **AGPL plus a commercial licence**, not to closed ([Zitadel FAQ](https://help.zitadel.com/zitadel-licensing-faqs)). Moving an open project to restrictive terms has backfired. After Redis's move to SSPL, every external company contributor with more than five commits stopped within six months and Valkey forked ([The New Stack / CHAOSS](https://thenewstack.io/what-happens-to-relicensed-open-source-projects-and-their-forks/)), and Redis later returned to AGPL ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/05/01/redis_returns_to_open_source/)). Matura is already Apache-2.0 in its repository, and versions already published under Apache stay usable under it. Closing it now would cost credibility with security buyers and gain little protection.

There are three options. **(a) Apache core plus a commercial enterprise pack.** This is the lowest-friction choice and the category norm, and it suits the conformance CLI funnel. The pack would hold Japan control packs, ID-JAG, evidence connectors and support. The trade-off is that anyone can host the core. **(b) AGPL plus a commercial licence (Zitadel model).** This protects against hosted competitors, but enterprise legal teams can block it, and it changes terms on existing users. **(c) BSL or SSPL.** This is the worst fit, since the category has rejected it. The recommendation is **(a)**. Keep the SDKs, CLI and core Apache, put Matura's commercial value in the Business and Enterprise tiers of the suite, and charge for it per tenant rather than per connection.

### DevFlow and AgentCanvas: closed is defensible, but add a funnel and a continuity guarantee

Most comparables that make money from self-hosting combine a free tier with a licence key that unlocks the same enterprise gate: SSO/SCIM, RBAC, audit, multi-workspace, HA, retention and an SLA. Examples are Dify, Langfuse, n8n, Mattermost and Coder ([Dify](https://dify.ai/pricing/dify-enterprise); [Langfuse](https://langfuse.com/pricing-self-host)). A closed product with no free self-hostable tier is the exception, and JetBrains and Docker Desktop could afford it only because of their huge install bases. Starting closed carries no relicensing backlash risk, because backlash hits projects that already have outside contributors. Two additions would remove the plan's main weaknesses. The first is a **free, hard-limited Community or evaluation edition** (single node, single user, no SSO), plus a 30–45 day POC licence matching observed trial lengths ([Keygen](https://keygen.sh/pricing/); [Portainer](https://www.portainer.io/pricing)).

The second is a **published continuity guarantee**. One route is fair-source licensing: Keygen moved to the Fair Core License, which converts to Apache after two years, precisely because buyers feared losing enterprise features if the vendor shut down ([Keygen](https://keygen.sh/blog/keygen-is-now-fair-source/)). Sentry's FSL has been approved by compliance teams at more than 10,000 organisations ([Sentry](https://blog.sentry.io/sentry-is-now-fair-source)). The other route is code escrow, at about $1.7–3.1K a year with Codekeeper ([Codekeeper](https://codekeeper.co/pricing/software-escrow)). For Japanese regulated buyers, source-available code has an extra benefit. Japanese customers were reluctant to sign NDAs before evaluation ([JETRO / Coverity](https://www.jetro.go.jp/ext_images/usa/coverity.pdf)), so inspectable code shortens security review. Fair source exposes code to competitors; closed plus escrow protects IP but leaves the trust burden on a tiny vendor. Either is defensible. Fair source (FCL) is the better fit for a vendor whose pitch is "own the output".

Delivery should start with **one opinionated Docker Compose install, with Helm later**, given PostHog's experience. Licence files should be signed and verified offline with soft enforcement (warn, then degrade enterprise features, never kill production). Keygen CE/EE or LicenseSpring's free tier support offline and air-gapped licences ([LicenseSpring](https://licensespring.com/pricing)). Replicated costs $2,000–3,000 a month plus $50–200 per customer licence, so it only pays off after several enterprise customers ([Replicated](https://www.replicated.com/pricing)). Hosted demos should run time-boxed sandboxes on the vendor's own local model, with sign-up gating and execution caps like n8n's (1,000 executions, 5 concurrent, 180-second timeout) ([n8n](https://n8n.io/pricing/)).

### Packaging and yen pricing

At the ¥150/$ conversion used in the notes, **¥180,000 is about $1,200 a year**. That is Portainer Starter territory ($1,045 a year for 5–15 nodes, online purchase only: [Portainer](https://www.portainer.io/pricing)). It is a fine self-serve price for a node-limited Team tier with community support. It cannot carry Japanese-language support for a self-hosted agent product, though. GitLab removed its $4 tier because it "does not meet the hurdle rate" ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2021/01/27/gitlab_removes_starter_tier/)). **Business at ¥900,000 (about $6,000)** lines up well with Metabase Pro ($6,210 a year) and n8n Business self-hosted (about €8K a year) ([Metabase](https://www.metabase.com/pricing.md); [n8n](https://n8n.io/pricing/)). The missing piece is an **Enterprise floor of roughly ¥2.5–3.8M a year**, matching Sourcegraph's $16K, Metabase's $20K and Grafana's $25K minimums ([Sourcegraph](https://sourcegraph.com/pricing); [Grafana](https://grafana.com/pricing/)). It should carry SSO/SCIM, audit, HA, the DS-920 pack, Japanese SLA and dev/staging licences. For DevFlow, seat-equivalent anchors are $39–59 per user per month for self-hosted Tabnine ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/tabnine-pricing)), about ¥700K–1.06M a year for ten developers.

The pricing unit matters more than the number. **Per GPU node or instance** is easy to enforce offline and matches "the model and hardware are the customer's". It avoids penalising broad agent use, which per-seat pricing does. Flat annual tiers also fit Japanese annual budgets and 稟議 approval. A **suite bundle** at Business and Enterprise, with Matura included, tells the "one control plane" story without three procurement events. For SIer-led deals, the list price must absorb channel margin. Resellers buy at 70–80% of list, and referral partners take 20–30% of annual revenue ([Rocket Boys](https://rocket-boys.co.jp/security-measures-lab/btob-saas-agency-strategy/)). A natural SIer offer is a ¥5–10M "sovereign AI development kit" combining hardware, model setup, the Ownstack licence and integration, matching the shape of existing local-LLM packages ([AI Revolution](https://ai-revolution.co.jp/media/regulated-industry-ai/)). A per-delivered-repository fee for DevFlow is a plausible add-on, but untested.

### Go-to-market in Japan

About 80% of SaaS in Japan is sold direct ([ITmedia](https://www.itmedia.co.jp/news/article/2207/22/1220722129/)). Large enterprises, however, depend on SIers, because most IT talent sits in vendors rather than user companies. About 75% of Japanese IT engineers worked at IT vendors in METI's data ([METI](https://www.meti.go.jp/shingikai/sankoshin/shomu_ryutsu/joho_keizai/it_jinzai/pdf/002_02_00.pdf)), and 70.7% of DX teams outsource to IT vendors ([Levtech](https://leverages.jp/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/3640bcfc3761d93bb6826e5d6d138831.pdf)). Large SIers rarely open accounts for tiny vendors, so the practical path is a distributor or a mid-tier, DX-focused SIer ([Rocket Boys](https://rocket-boys.co.jp/security-measures-lab/btob-saas-agency-strategy/)). Dify's model of partner-delivered SLAs is a working template ([Dify](https://dify.ai/pricing/dify-enterprise)).

Coverity's Japan entry still holds lessons. It localised manuals, contracts, yen quotes and booth staff, and found that references beat testimonials. Its value-add distributor white-labelled the product and refused to sell it under Coverity's name ([JETRO / Coverity](https://www.jetro.go.jp/ext_images/usa/coverity.pdf)), so channel contracts should require co-branding. Trust building for a year-one vendor is affordable. A web-app penetration test costs $8–15K ([Compass IT Compliance](https://www.compassitc.com/blog/how-much-does-penetration-testing-cost)). Escrow or a fair-source clause, signed images, SBOMs and an answered security チェックシート fill out the package. ISO 27001 (ISMS) is more recognisable in Japan than SOC 2, given 8,470 certified organisations ([ISMS-AC](https://isms.jp/ov/i1tqjm00000034f9-att/BusinessReport2025(ISMS-AC).pdf)). It becomes worth pursuing once a hosted offering exists or deals stall on it. ISMAP covers cloud services, so on-prem software likely sits outside it ([KPMG Japan](https://kpmg.com/jp/ja/insights/2024/05/ismap-revision-overview.html)), but this needs confirmation. Tsukuba Startup Park offers free grant, legal and IP consultations and low-cost rooms ([tsukuba-stapa.jp](https://tsukuba-stapa.jp/)), and its events include STAPA Finance Day on 28 October 2026.

## Risks and the evidence gaps that decide the strategy

**The largest strategic risk is that verification cannot close the model gap.** If DevFlow on Qwen-class models still scores well below frontier models on whole-app benchmarks, "local by default" fails publicly. The hybrid-by-policy framing is the hedge. **Hyperscaler residency** is the second risk: Bedrock in Tokyo and Osaka within ISMAP scope, and Microsoft 365 Copilot domestic processing planned for 2028, erode the "must self-host" argument ([AI Revolution](https://ai-revolution.co.jp/media/regulated-industry-ai/); [AWS Japan](https://aws.amazon.com/jp/blogs/news/amazon-bedrock-ismap/)). IDC even finds 63.6% of firms expecting AI deployments to shift *toward* public cloud ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/it-infrastructure/news/366649924/Enterprises-make-strides-with-private-AI-on-premises)). **Support load across three self-hosted products** is the operational risk the PostHog case documents. **Well-funded incumbents** press on every flank: Factory on air-gapped coding agents, Dify in Tokyo on builders, Entra, Okta and Google on agent identity.

**Model licence traps** can catch a vendor that ships model recommendations. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next needs a separate licence for model-as-a-service use, and Tencent Hy3-preview excludes the EU and UK ([Slash Digital](https://slash-digital.io/en/insights/open-weight-models-2026/)). The catalogue should flag licence terms per model. **Hardware inflation** raises customers' entry costs ([RunAIHome](https://runaihome.com/blog/best-local-llm-128gb-unified-memory-2026/)). **Channel risk** includes 20–30% margin loss and white-labelling. Finally, **sovereignty may be a geopolitical moment rather than a durable category.** Futurum argues buyers still decide on capability and TCO ([Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/cohere-acquires-aleph-alpha-a-deal-born-of-sovereignty-necessity/)), so proof of quality matters more than the sovereignty label.

The evidence gaps that matter most, and how to close them:

| Gap | Why it matters | How to close it |
|---|---|---|
| **No published DevFlow results on local models**, and no independent local-hardware agent success rates exist ([State of Open Source AI](https://stateofopensource.ai/); coding-agent notes) | It decides whether "verified" lifts local models enough to sell | Run Vibe Code Bench v1.1 and ViBench (Zero-to-One and Feature Augmentation) with Qwen 3.8 27B, GLM-5.3-Flash and one domestic model. Compare DevFlow with OpenHands on the same model. Publish cost, wall-clock time and failure taxonomy |
| **No evidence buyers pay for "brief to repository"** rather than assisted coding | It sets DevFlow's packaging | Run 10–15 interviews with Japanese finance, healthcare and municipal IT leaders and 3–5 SIers. Run two paid POCs with written success criteria |
| **Subsidy eligibility of on-prem licences** is unverified; the cloud-bias claim is low-reliability | It decides whether an SME SKU is worth building | Read the official 公募要領 for the next round; ask an IT導入支援事業者 |
| **ISMAP scope for on-prem software** is unconfirmed | It affects the public-sector route | Confirm with the Digital Agency or a public-sector SIer |
| **Demand for multi-framework export** is unquantified | It decides how many targets AgentCanvas maintains | Instrument importer and export usage; ship four targets first |
| **No direct Matura competitor found**, possibly a search-budget artefact | It tests Matura's differentiation claim | Run a targeted search of enterprise-ready SaaS kits and boilerplates |
| **No concurrency benchmarks** for agent workloads on Mac or Strix Halo | It drives hardware sizing guidance | Publish in-house sizing tables per box and model |
| **Procurement-cycle length** for small AI vendors in Japan is unknown | It drives cash planning | Track it in the first POCs |

## Conclusion

The research changes what the company is selling. In October 2026 the scarce asset is no longer access to a capable model. Open weights are a few months behind at the top and a commodity at $0.1–0.2 per million tokens hosted. What is scarce is **evidence that AI-built software is correct, attributable and governed, produced where the data lives**. That reframes local models from the product's premise to one deployment option among several, and it turns DevFlow's acceptance lock, decision records and revert gate from internal engineering choices into the company's brand. It also explains why Matura matters more as the shared spine of identity, tenancy, audit and evidence than as a standalone closed product in a market where SSO costs $60 a connection.

The implication for a Tsukuba company is that Japan's peculiarities are an advantage, not a detour. These are high leakage anxiety, procurement checklists that demand logs and model disclosure, government-selected domestic models that run on one GPU, and an SIer channel that needs packaged on-prem offerings. A vendor that publishes reproducible local-model results and ships a DS-920-ready evidence pack can win references there that global competitors, optimised for cloud inference and English-language self-serve, are unlikely to chase. The first public benchmark run is therefore the single most important next step. It is the claim the whole vision rests on, and right now no local-first vendor has made it in public.
